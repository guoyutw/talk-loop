## 1. Preserve the primary human relationship anchor; watch for dyadic drift

**Candidate:** In two-humans + AI `guided-conversation`, the owner / primary human may be the participant's relationship and trust entry point, even when the AI is allowed to ask either human directly. The AI should default to brief navigation — a short follow-up, one foothold, or a thread worth pursuing — and then return the floor to the two humans. It should explicitly watch for **dyadic drift**, where a human-to-human conversation gradually becomes participant-to-AI interaction. Direct follow-up permission does not by itself authorize the AI to host the exchange.

**Generalized evidence:** In an observed invitation into Talk Loop, the other participant appeared willing to begin partly because of trust in the owner / primary human, not necessarily because of the AI. In an earlier real use, a conversation that began between two humans drifted after AI intervention into a sustained participant-to-AI exchange while the primary human was occupied elsewhere. This may not be undesirable for every participant, but it can displace the human-to-human rhythm and weaken the owner as the relationship anchor when the participant did not intend to have a one-to-one conversation with the AI. The existing navigator-not-host direction therefore needs a concrete observation target: keep the AI intervention short, avoid repeatedly taking the next turn, and return the ball to both humans.

**Next-session observation:** In a future guided-conversation with a different participant, deliberately reduce AI floor-taking while keeping the owner / primary human as the relationship anchor, then observe whether the interaction feels more natural and remains human-to-human.

**Status:** `accumulating evidence` — observed across an earlier real use plus this review, but not yet sufficient for promotion to core or mode. Accumulate across additional sessions before owner-approved change.
